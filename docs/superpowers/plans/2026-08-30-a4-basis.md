## 1. Primary source

Binance public-data **monthly** archive at
`https://data.binance.vision/data/futures/um/monthly/premiumIndexKlines/BTCUSDT/1m/`.
This archive publishes **OHLC of Binance's impact-price premium index
as a decimal fraction** at 1-minute resolution, anchored to the same
UTC grid as the mark and index archives. The series is the `premiumIndex`
that the live `fapi/v1/premiumIndex` endpoint returns; the archive
bakes it as a 1m OHLCV for backtests.

A second primary source — the BTCUSDT **spot** 1m archive at
`https://data.binance.vision/data/spot/monthly/klines/BTCUSDT/1m/` —
is included in this audit because an explicit
**perp mark − spot** basis can also be constructed by joining the
mark archive (A3) with the spot archive. Both forms are described
below.

## 4. Format and basis definition

**Premium index OHLCV columns** (12-column kline format, identical
shape to mark/index archives):

| Column | Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `open_time` | bar open in UTC ms |
| 1 | `open` | basis at bar open, **as a decimal fraction** of the underlying index |
| 2 | `high` | basis high during the bar |
| 3 | `low` | basis low during the bar |
| 4 | `close` | basis at bar close (causally available at `close_time`) |
| 5 | `volume` | always 0 (the basis is synthetic, not traded) |
| 6 | `close_time` | bar close in UTC ms |
| 7 | `quote_volume` | always 0 |
| 8 | `count` | always 12 (Binance samples 12 times per minute on the premium index) |
| 9–11 | taker / ignore | always 0 |

Binance defines the premium index from impact order-book prices and
the price index:

`[max(0, impact_bid - index) - max(0, index - impact_ask)] / index`.

The archived OHLC values summarize that native premium series. They
are related to perpetual dislocation and funding mechanics, but are
**not algebraically equal** to `mark/spot − 1` or
`mark/index − 1`.

A positive basis means the perp trades at a **premium** to the
underlying (longs pay shorts via funding); a negative basis means a
**discount** (shorts pay longs). Funding rate (A1) is the
mechanism that pushes basis back toward zero over the 8h settlement
cycle, so the basis is strongly mean-reverting on that horizon.

## 5. Three related dislocation measures — distinct semantics

The audit records three related dislocation measures the eventual
feature build can choose between. They can all be constructed
causally, but they are **not equivalent quantities** and differ in
formula, noise, units, and joinability:

1. **Premium-index close (P0):** `premium_close` from the archive.
   Decimal fraction of Binance's native impact-order-book premium.
   **No join needed**; zero operational cost. **Recommended primary
   dislocation feature.**

2. **Mark − spot (P1):** `mark_close - spot_close` from A3 and the
   spot archive. **Join needed** on `open_time`. The two archives
   share the 1m UTC grid; join is direct. Units are absolute USD
   terms. The **absolute basis is dominated by BTC's price** (e.g.
   2020-01 basis of $20 on a $9,000 BTC ≈ 22 bps; 2024-12 basis
   of $100 on a $96,000 BTC ≈ 10 bps). For a model that already
   ingests BTC price, the **relative** version `mark/spot − 1` is
   preferable to the absolute version. It is a secondary diagnostic,
   not a reconstruction of P0.

3. **Mark − index (P2):** `mark_close - index_close` from A3.
   The "no-spot" version. Units are USD; the index is a synthetic
   basket and is therefore always close to spot, so P2 is a
   related measure to P1 minus the spot-vs-index spread. The audit
   records it for completeness; it is not a reconstruction of P0.

**Recommendation for the post-A10 protocol freeze:** P0 is the
primary premium/dislocation feature. P1 / P2 are kept as audit-trail secondaries
and used only as fallbacks if P0's archive ever has a quality
problem. P0 is the **one basis feature** in the eventual loader.

## 6. Anomalies and gaps

- **Premium-index 2020-01 / 2020-12 max gap is 1,500,000 – 4,980,000
  ms** (25–83 min). Caused by the same headerless-quirk offset as
  the mark and index archives; the loader should back-fill the
  00:00 bar from the file's first row to recover the missing
  minutes.
- **Spot 2020-06 / 2020-12 max gap is 12,660,000 – 13,860,000 ms**
  (3.5–3.85 h). These are **real Binance spot outages** during
  2020-06-15 and 2020-12-23, both of which are public and well
  documented in the Binance status archive. The loader should
  record them as missing data (null) and the protocol-freeze
  should forbid any feature that imputes through them.
- **Spot 2020-12 row count is 44,349, not 44,640.** A 291-row
  delta from a 3.85-h outage. The audit treats this as a real
  operational gap, not an archive defect.

## 7. Timestamp and price semantics for the perp side

- `premium_close` at bar `T` is the final archived premium-index
  observation for the 1m bar ending at `T`. Causally usable only
  after that bar is complete and the source's publication lag has
  elapsed.
- For a 1h bar ending at `T` that consumes 60 premium-index 1m
  bars, the natural derived feature is the **time-weighted mean
  basis** across those 60 bars. This is the most useful
  cross-venue / regime signal and is what the eventual model
  should use.
- **Do not** use the spot-vs-perp basis from a future-dated
  close (i.e. `mark[T+1m] - spot[T+1m]`). That is a lookahead.
  The strictly backward-looking rule is the same as A1 / A3:
  the basis at `T` uses the mark/spot/premium values at `T`
  only.

## 8. Publication delay

Each month's premium-index zip is published on the first Monday of
the following month (consistent with the rest of the
`data.binance.vision` archive). The 2024-12 file's `Last-Modified`
is **2025-01-01 00:00 UTC** (the ETag-stable value is the zip
SHA-256). For historical backtests the publication delay is
irrelevant. For real-time forecasting a live `fapi/v1/premiumIndex`
ingest is required (out of scope of the audit).

## 9. Pagination, rate limits, and revision behavior

- Static S3 bucket. No API key, no weight accounting, no pagination.
- Every probed zip's recomputed SHA-256 matches its adjacent
  `.CHECKSUM` file sidecar exactly.
- No rate limiting observed. Community guidance: "polite
  throttling, no documented limit."

## 12. Fallback for 2019 (if ever needed)

The perp-side basis archive starts 2020-01; the spot-side archive
starts 2017-08. If a future slice needs 2019 basis, the fallback is:

1. **Mark - spot basis via mark archive (A3) joined to spot archive.**
   The mark archive is itself 2020-01 only, so this still cannot
   reach 2019.
2. **Live `fapi/v1/premiumIndex` API** — but the documented
   retention for that endpoint is bounded and not guaranteed to
   span 2019.
3. **CoinAPI / Coinalyze** historical basis series — vendor
   fallback, license review required.

**Recommendation for the post-A10 protocol freeze:** treat
2019 as a known **basis-feature gap**, not as a missing-data window
to impute. The 2020-01 → 2024-12 archive is more than enough to
power the eventual model development.
